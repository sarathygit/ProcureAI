The problem

Picking the cheapest quote is how bad purchases happen.

Procurement teams compare quotations by hand, under time pressure, across inconsistent PDFs and spreadsheets.

🧩

Missing specifications

A quote that never states its warranty or delivery time quietly gets "assumed" compliant.

🕳️

Hidden costs

Freight "at actuals", installation extra, 6-month battery cover — buried in the fine print.

🎭

Fraud & collusion

Invalid GST numbers, too-good-to-be-true prices and copy-pasted rival bids go unnoticed.

How it works

Rules decide. Humans approve.

Every compliance check, cost calculation and ranking is deterministic, explainable and repeatable — so the decision can be defended to an auditor.

📄

Read

PDF & Excel quotations are parsed into structured data. Every value gets a confidence score and the sentence it came from.

✅

Features

Everything between "we need 20 laptops" and a signed PO.

📊

Compliance-first evaluation

Eligibility before price, strict landed-cost ranking, weighted composite score and trade-off insights such as "relax delivery by 2 days to save ₹94,600".

🔍

Extraction with confidence

Understands messy wording ("half a terabyte", "a fortnight"). Anything below 80% confidence waits for human confirmation.

🛡️

Fraud & hidden-cost detection

GSTIN checksum validation, below-market pricing, fine-print extras and bid-collusion detection between suppliers.

📉

Supplier risk score

Delivery history, defect rate and disputes feed a 0–100 risk score and a risk-adjusted cost.

🤝

Negotiation agent

Multi-round negotiation against simulated supplier personas with hidden limits. It never accepts a deal on its own.

🧮

Decision lab

Total cost of ownership, what-if simulator and a capacity-aware split-order optimizer.

📜

Policy & approval chain

Minimum quotes, MSME preference, blacklist and risk rules; approvals scale with order value.

💬

Chat-based requests

"Two dozen laptops, 32 gigs, by end of next week, 85k each" becomes a structured requirement.
